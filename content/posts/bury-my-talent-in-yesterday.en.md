You have probably seen the line floating around: the future of the AI age has only two directions, communism or Cyberpunk 2077. It sounds like a meme. It actually comes from a late-night essay by a DeepSeek engineer.

![Cover illustration: an afternoon of writing code on the left, a mecha pilot on the right (AI-redrawn)](https://oss.hao430.cn/wechat/lsy-bury-talent/20260916/09-cover-new-a8ca39.webp)

His name is Liu Shengyu (刘胜与). Turing Class of Peking University, captain of the university's supercomputing team, winner of the international student supercomputing competition, and the author of the main Attention kernel in DeepSeek V4.1. In the early hours of September 14, he published a long post titled *I Have No Choice But to Bury My Talent in Yesterday* (《我不得不把才华埋葬在昨天》). Overnight it went viral: over 100,000 reads on WeChat, first on Zhihu's hot list, heavy discussion on X, and translations into several languages.

![Original title and author attribution](https://oss.hao430.cn/wechat/lsy-bury-talent/20260916/01-title-c136b0.png)

Most people remember the first half: a craftsman saying goodbye to his craft.

He did the math. A year ago, AI was a little assistant that looked up documentation, read code, and found bugs. Today it can read CUDA, PTX and SASS on its own, analyze the stall time of every instruction with professional tools, and optimize kernels by itself. His estimate: within six months to a year, AI-written kernels will match or exceed his own. And the math is a closed loop. The better his kernels, the faster the model trains and runs, the faster AI improves, the sooner he is replaced.

He will probably not be unemployed. But he will have to change professions, from writing kernels by hand to piloting AI agents, a mecha pilot, in his words. What he cannot let go of is not the job. It is the pleasure. His metaphor: a person who knits beautiful sweaters, sitting by the window for a whole afternoon with tea and mountains outside. Then a machine arrives that knits just as well and far faster. Your craft survives, and with twenty years of skill you still beat your peers even on the same machine. But that afternoon is crushed under the noise of the machine. There are more gears in my hands now, he writes, but fewer beats in my heart.

![Original screenshot: humanity destroying itself](https://oss.hao430.cn/wechat/lsy-bury-talent/20260916/03-self-revolution-f543e0.png)

He wrote: of course I hope I won't be swept away by the revolution. But if I have to be, I would rather the person who revolutionizes me be myself.

Yet he says the heavier part comes later.

After doing the math on his own career, he did the math for everyone. As AI develops, he argues, society will tend toward one of two extremes. In the first, communism, productive forces are greatly liberated and living standards rise across the board. In the second, Cyberpunk 2077, a handful of technology companies control most of the world's resources; only a tiny minority gets to use the strongest AI, approaching mechanical ascension, while most people are left with weak, crippled AI. Class mobility grinds to a halt. To move up you first need the strongest AI, and the strongest AI is held by the people already at the top. A dead loop. He stops writing there, joking that continuing might not pass review.

![Original screenshot: communism and Cyberpunk 2077](https://oss.hao430.cn/wechat/lsy-bury-talent/20260916/05-communism-vs-2077-1cd735.png)

That is why he believes frontier intelligence should be provided to everyone in an open, inexpensive way. He does not trust Anthropic or OpenAI to do this. He goes further: if Anthropic permanently controlled the world's most advanced AI, he writes, exaggerating slightly, it would be comparable to Hitler obtaining the atomic bomb before the Allies.

This is deeply contested, and he knows it. He is not a historian or a sociologist, he admits; those passages are asides, not the center of the essay, and they represent his personal view, not his employer's. But two sentences he insists are sincere: one of the reasons he chose DeepSeek is that he does not want the world to end up like 2077, and the closing line, May all the beauty be blessed.

He stayed at DeepSeek because the lab researches powerful, fast, broadly accessible AI and open-sources it. Perhaps that can pull the world a little farther away from the 2077 end of the spectrum. Hobby is one thing, conviction another. A man who is burying his own talent by hand, still trying to leave the strongest intelligence to everyone. I believe him.

![Original screenshot: the tip line at the end of the essay](https://oss.hao430.cn/wechat/lsy-bury-talent/20260916/06-tip-joke-2a194a.png)

After the essay exploded, some saw the twilight of the craftsman. I saw something else. The most frightening part of 2077 is not that machines take jobs. It is that most people end up with weak AI. The most plain counter-move is available right now.

For the first time in history, the strongest models are free for ordinary people, and DeepSeek itself is open source. Nobody knows how long this window of free frontier intelligence will last. The free, strongest tool in your hand is your ticket. Practice it until it becomes part of how you work. Do not wait until the day you are handed a crippled version, and realize you once had a chance.

In his follow-up post he wrote that after the rain, he will still use a warmed heart to clear the haze, looking for the blue that appears after the night.
